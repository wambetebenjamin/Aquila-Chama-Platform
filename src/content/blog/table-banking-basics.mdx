Table banking is simple enough to explain in one sentence and difficult enough
to run well for years. Members sit around a table, contribute a fixed amount at
every meeting, and lend the pooled money to members at a modest interest rate.
The genius is not the mechanics. The genius is that the collateral is
relationship, not property.

## The three pots

A well-run table banking group separates its money into three pots from the
first meeting:

- **Savings** — the contributions themselves. This is the members' money and it
  must always be recoverable.
- **Loans** — money currently out with members earning interest.
- **Social** — a small optional fund for welfare events, funerals and
  celebrations.

Mixing the second pot into the first is where groups get into trouble. When
loans outstanding grow faster than savings, the group is lending money that a
member may ask for back next week.

> Never lend out more than seventy per cent of what is saved. The remaining
> thirty per cent is what keeps the group honest when three people need their
> savings at once.

## Meeting rhythm

Weekly or fortnightly meetings work better than monthly ones. Short, frequent
meetings keep contribution discipline high and let the group spot a struggling
borrower early, when a conversation still solves the problem.

## The lending window

Give loans a fixed term and a fixed repayment date. Six to twelve months is
typical. Members who repay early should be allowed to borrow again immediately;
that is the incentive that keeps the whole system turning.

## Interest: keep it boring

Ten per cent per year is common and works. The purpose of interest is not to
make the group rich. It is to make lending slightly more attractive than
borrowing, so that money keeps moving and the pool keeps growing.

## Where groups get it wrong

1. **Lending to non-members.** Resist it. A non-member has no savings at risk.
2. **Rolling over a defaulting loan.** Rolling over hides the problem for one
   term and doubles it the next.
3. **Letting one person hold both the cash and the record.** Separate the roles.
   A treasurer and a secretary who check each other cost nothing.
4. **Skipping minutes.** Minutes are not admin. They are the memory of the
   group, and they are what settles disputes eighteen months later.

## Record it as you go

A paper register can work, but it cannot tell you in ten seconds who is
overdue, what the distributable amount is, or what each member's dividend will
be at year end. That is the difference between a group that survives its third
year and a group that quietly stops meeting in February.
