Every chama in Kenya has a story about the meeting that went badly. Two members
disagree about a balance, somebody mentions a loan from 2023, and the afternoon
is gone. In almost every case the argument is not really about the money. It is
about the fact that the money was never written down in a place everyone trusts.

Here are the seven rules that remove most of the friction.

## 1. Write the contribution amount down, once

A fixed monthly amount removes an entire category of debate. When the amount is
KES 5,000, nobody negotiates in February because January was expensive. Pick the
figure at registration, record it, and change it only by a vote at a meeting
with minutes.

## 2. Record on the day, not at the meeting

The longer the gap between a payment and its record, the fuzzier the memory.
Recording on the same day means the M-Pesa confirmation message is still on the
treasurer's phone. Aquila Chama Platform lets a treasurer log a payment with the
reference code in under thirty seconds.

## 3. Every loan needs two guarantors

One guarantor is a favour. Two guarantors are a system. With two people
accountable, recovery stops being a personal confrontation and becomes a group
process. Both guarantors should vote on the platform before any money moves.

## 4. Agree the interest rate before the first loan

Arguments about interest are arguments about expectations that were never set.
Decide the rate at registration, write it into the settings, and let the
platform calculate the repayment amount. Most Kenyan chamas sit between eight
and twelve per cent per year.

## 5. Share the statement before the meeting

When members receive the statement on WhatsApp a day early, the meeting is
about decisions instead of discovery. The treasurer stops reading numbers aloud
and the group gets ninety minutes back.

## 6. Decide what happens when someone misses three months

Every group eventually faces this. Agree the rule while everybody is friendly.
Suspension from borrowing is the common answer, and it should be automatic
rather than personal.

## 7. Keep one register of members

A single, current register with correct phone numbers solves more problems than
any other document. When a number changes, update it that week. Reminders, loan
votes and minutes all depend on it.

> A chama does not fail because members stop trusting each other. It fails
> because they stop trusting the numbers. Fix the numbers first.

None of these rules require discipline that members do not have. They require a
system that makes the right thing the easiest thing. That is the whole point of
managing a chama on a platform instead of in a exercise book.
